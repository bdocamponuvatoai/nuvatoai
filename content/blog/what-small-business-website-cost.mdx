A small business website can mean two different purchases. One is a monthly plan for a template you fill in and look after yourself. The other is a system built around how your business takes enquiries, and the price for that is set once the job is written down.

This post is for owners of businesses of about ten to twenty people in the US, Canada and Argentina. Each section stands on its own, so read the one you need.

## What does a monthly website plan pay for?

A website builder plan pays for the template, the hosting and the tools to edit the pages. You choose a design, write the words, add the photos and keep it up to date.

For many businesses that is the right purchase. If the site needs to show who you are, what you do and how to reach you, a plan does that job.

What the plan does not include is your process. It does not know who answers the contact form, where a new enquiry is recorded or what happens when nobody replies by Friday.

## What does a built system pay for?

A system is the website plus what happens after someone uses it.

The form on the page. The record the form writes to. The reminder that goes out when an enquiry is waiting. The person who checks the ones that do not fit. The written procedure that says who does what.

It is built once, for how your business works, and kept running. The website is the part a visitor sees. The rest is the part your team uses every day.

## How do you know which one your business needs?

Ask what happens when someone fills in your contact form at nine in the evening.

If the answer is that someone sees it the next morning and replies, a monthly plan may be all you need.

If the answer is that it depends who checks the inbox, or that enquiries sometimes sit for days, the website is not the problem. The process behind it is. A better template will not fix that.

A second question helps. How many places does one customer's information live? If it is copied by hand from the form to a spreadsheet to a calendar, each copy is a place for it to go wrong.

## What should you ask before you pay for either one?

Whoever you talk to, a few questions make the difference clear.

- **What exactly is included?** A plan lists its features. A build should come with a written scope that says what will exist when the work is finished, in words you can check.
- **Who looks after it afterwards?** With a plan, you do. With a built system, ask who you call when something stops working, and whether the same team built the website, the forms and the automations behind them.
- **What happens to your information?** Ask where enquiries are stored, who can see them and how you get them out if you ever leave.
- **What is the price, and when is it set?** A monthly plan has a published price. For a build, ask whether the price is fixed before work starts or estimated and adjusted as the job goes.
- **Can you see a real example?** Ask for a system the team has built and can show with the owner's permission. A demo built on sample data is useful too, as long as it is labelled as one.

None of these questions favours one answer. They make sure you know which purchase you are making.

## Why should the price be fixed before work starts?

A price needs something to attach to. That something is a written scope: what is being built, for whom, and what finished looks like.

When the scope is written first, both sides read the same page. The owner knows what is included before agreeing to anything. The team building it knows where the job ends.

When a price is set without a scope, the scope gets decided during the work. That is where most surprises come from, for the owner and for the builder.
